B2B FCL quote guide · Cost scope

What Should a Container Shipping Quote Include?

A useful FCL quote tells your business which transport and handling tasks are included. This guide helps importers and exporters compare the same pickup-to-delivery scope. It does not publish a freight rate: prices depend on the actual cargo, route, readiness, equipment, and requested responsibilities.

Compare the same start and end points

A port-to-port freight figure and a collection-to-delivery offer cover different work. State the actual pickup location, intended handoff, receiving address, and services you want included. If commercial terms have already been agreed with the supplier or buyer, include them in the request so the quote can reflect that arrangement.

Ask which costs are included, which are excluded, and which depend on a later shipment event or a third-party requirement. A comparison is more useful when the proposals describe the same load, dates, equipment and service scope.

Tasks to define in a commercial container quote
Part of the shipmentQuestion to clarify
Origin collectionWhere does collection start, and who releases and loads the goods?
Container and handlingWhich equipment, loading and origin handling tasks are included?
Main freightWhat route and service are being quoted for the specified load?
Destination tasksWhich port, documentation and delivery arrangements are included?

Why cargo details change the comparison

The number of containers is not the only input. Dimensions, gross weight, packaging, stacking restrictions and loading method can change the equipment and handling requirement. Machinery may need preparation or site handling that ordinary boxed stock does not.

For a single bulky item from another origin, ask whether an FCL arrangement is practical and compare the full scope. Dedicated container use does not require every cubic metre to be occupied. Our published LCL offer is limited to seven selected origins into Lebanon.

Prepare the details before requesting rates

  • Your company, actual pickup point and final receiving point.
  • Cargo description, packed dimensions, weights and container requirement.
  • Readiness date, required arrival window and any agreed shipment frequency.
  • Loading, unloading, lifting, documentation and delivery tasks requested.
  • Commercial handoff terms already agreed and responsibilities still to be decided.

Check the assumptions before accepting an offer

Read the quoted scope, validity and stated conditions. Confirm that the goods will be ready in the described transport condition and that both sites can provide the agreed access and handling. Ask how a change in cargo, dates, equipment or site requirements would be reviewed.

The freight desk confirms the proposed arrangement for the actual shipment. Customs outcomes, live carrier space, routing and final transit timing are not determined by this guide or by a calculator result.

Business freight questions

Can you publish a single FCL rate for every Lebanon shipment?

No. A useful quote needs the actual origin and destination, cargo, container requirement, readiness and requested service scope. This guide explains cost scope rather than offering a fixed rate.

What is the difference between a freight rate and total shipment scope?

A freight rate may describe only one transport leg. The full scope can also involve collection, loading, handling, documentation coordination, destination tasks and delivery where requested.

Is LCL always cheaper for a smaller business load?

No. Compare the complete collection, consolidation, handling and delivery scope for the same shipment. LCL is offered only from our selected origins into Lebanon.

What should we send for an FCL cost review?

Send company details, pickup and delivery points, a packing list with dimensions and gross weights, container needs, readiness date and requested handling and delivery tasks.
